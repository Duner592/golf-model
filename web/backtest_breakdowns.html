Methodology and limitations
  • Final preferred uses the latest saved prediction from 6pm to midnight in the course’s local time on the day before the event starts. The local-midnight cutoff is deliberately earlier than the first tee time; no opening-day runs qualify. Capture requires a known timezone and a fresh prediction timestamp, and scheduled runs can be delayed or fail.
  • If no final snapshot exists, preferred mode falls back to the initial archive. Use the snapshot selector to compare initial-only and final-only samples; these may contain different events. The coverage table identifies the source and timestamp. Final snapshots evaluate near-start accuracy, not necessarily the predictions available when an earlier bet was placed.
  • Only completed events qualify. Initial snapshots require a recorded creation time before the start date (00:00 UTC). Reconstructed or unverified predictions are excluded. Older events are not recreated as final snapshots after play, and late field changes can still cause coverage exclusions.
  • Players are matched by normalized names and documented first-name aliases. Ambiguous matches are not guessed. Each metric requires at least 95% coverage of both predictions and results. Winner and top-10 scoring must include every observed positive outcome.
  • Percent probabilities are scored against binary outcomes. Top-10 ties count as top 10. Withdrawals/disqualifications count as non-wins/non-top-10s; cut outcomes remain unknown unless explicit. Events with no observed missed cut are excluded from make-cut scoring.
  • Each event has equal weight, including in calibration. Player counts are appearances, not unique people. Calibration gap is the weighted absolute difference in fixed 10-percentage-point bands. Brier uses exact probabilities; log loss clips at 0.000001.
  • The retrospective baseline assigns every player the observed outcome rate across all known results in that event (for example, one winner divided by field size). It is a no-discrimination reference, not bookmaker odds or a pre-event forecast. Both scores use the same evaluated players.
  • The 95% interval resamples whole events 1,000 times for the model-minus-baseline Brier difference. It is descriptive, assumes independent events, and is unavailable for one event. Repeated players and shared model versions limit that assumption. Groups with fewer than 10 events are labelled limited evidence.
  • Field size comes from archived tournament metadata: small <80, medium 80–119, full 120+. Majors are the Masters, PGA Championship, U.S. Open and Open Championship. Other eligible events are regular events. These splits do not control for field strength, course or changing model versions.
